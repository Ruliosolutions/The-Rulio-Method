Talking points

Use these as the spine of any interview or podcast. Each is paired with the contrarian take (so the audience hears both sides).

"The frequencies do the work."

The point is not to "do the meditation" or "force the focus." The 528 Hz + delta beat pulls the brain into the right state on its own. Trying is what stops the effect.

"5 minutes is enough."

Longer isn't better. The brain entrains in 90 seconds. 5 minutes is the minimum for it to feel different; 25 is the Pomodoro length; 60 is the full sleep cycle. Everything else is decoration.

"9 frequencies, not 1."

528 Hz is the famous one. It's also wrong for half your day. Sleep wants delta. Deep work wants low beta. Anxiety wants low alpha. One frequency per state, picked by the state you're actually in.

"This is not a meditation app."

Calm, Headspace, Waking Up, Muse — all meditation. Rulio is frequency math. The output is a written protocol, not a session. The point is to do less, not more.

"Not a medical device."

Every public surface has the disclaimer. No claims about treating conditions. The protocol is a relaxation tool for the operator who already has a sleep, focus, or anxiety pattern they want to shift.

"Built in Brussels, sold in 4 languages."

EN/NL/FR are first-class. DE is in the queue. The brand is founder-led, evidence-led, no buzzwords — no "manifest your reality," no "raise your vibration." Numbers, protocols, sleep latency.

What the press gets wrong (and how to get it right)

The most common mistake is framing Rulio as a wellness or meditation product. It's a frequency product. The brand guide calls this out in red:

  • ✓ "Adaptive audio for sleep, focus, and creative flow" — accurate
  • ✓ "Binaural-beat and solfeggio frequency sessions" — accurate
  • ✗ "Wellness app" — wrong; we're a frequency app
  • ✗ "Meditation tool" — wrong; we're not a meditation tool
  • ✗ "Sound healing" — wrong; we don't claim healing
  • ✗ "Vibration raising" — wrong; not part of the brand
