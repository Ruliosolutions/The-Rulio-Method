# Rulio — Social Content: First 7 Days

**Owner:** Roel Janssens · Brussels
**Channels:** Twitter/X + LinkedIn
**Window:** Day 1 (launch day) through Day 7 (end of week 1)
**Goal of week 1:** Establish the story. Earn the right to ask for a download. Not to pitch.

---

## How to use this

This file is 7 days of paste-ready posts. Every morning post, every thread, every LinkedIn essay, and every engagement prompt is written out below. You do not need to write anything yourself — you copy, you paste, you engage.

### Pre-launch checklist (do this before Day 1)

1. **Update your Twitter bio.** Something like: `Building Rulio · adaptive solfeggio audio for focus, sleep, energy · 14 free sessions · rulio.app`
2. **Update your LinkedIn headline.** `Founder @ Rulio · Adaptive audio for focus, sleep, energy · Brussels`
3. **Make sure both bios point to rulio.app** (not rulio.app/pro or any deep link — those come later).
4. **Prepare two bookmarks** in your browser: `rulio.app/pro` (the 7-day trial page) and `rulio.app/qi` (the free 14-session practice). You will need them from Day 3 onward.

### Posting times (Europe/Berlin)

- **Morning post (the main tweet):** 08:30 — 09:00. Knowledge workers open their phones at the kitchen counter. This is the highest-trust window of the day.
- **LinkedIn long-form:** 08:00 — 09:00. LinkedIn rewards early-morning posts that catch the 9 AM commute scroll.
- **Twitter thread:** post the first tweet at 09:00, the rest every 3-5 minutes. Thread tweets can be spaced over 30 minutes if you want each to land in a fresh timeline.
- **Engagement post (the question or poll):** 17:00 — 18:00. People check Twitter and LinkedIn as they wind down. Lower reach, higher reply rate. That is the trade we want.

### Engagement rules

- **Reply to every comment within 30 minutes** of the post going live. The first hour sets the algorithm's read of the post. If you go quiet, the post dies.
- **Reply to the negative comments first.** Not the angry ones — the skeptical ones. "I don't believe in this" is a better reply to get than "Great post!"
- **No deep links in Twitter for the first 3 days.** Twitter deprioritises posts with clickable links. The bio link is enough. The root domain `rulio.app` in plain text is fine (it is not a clickable hyperlink). Day 4 onward you can use `rulio.app/qi` and `rulio.app/pro` freely in the body.
- **No `rulio.app/pro` on LinkedIn before Day 3.** The trial pitch should arrive on Day 3 or later, after the story lands. `rulio.app/qi` (the free practice) is fine on Day 1 because it is not a pitch.
- **Pin the Day 1 post to your Twitter profile.** It is the only pitch you need for week 1.

### Voice reminders

- First person. "I" not "we." Rulio is a one-person launch.
- Short sentences. One idea per sentence.
- Honest. If you do not have evidence for a claim, do not make the claim.
- Vulnerable when it fits. Burnout is a story, not a sales hook.
- **No emojis in Days 1, 2, 3.** From Day 4 onward, one or two per post is fine. Do not go wild.
- **No hashtags in Days 1, 2, 3** of Twitter. Hashtags make the post look like a brand account. We are a person.
- **No buzzwords.** No "transformative," "revolutionary," "life-changing," "unlock," "elevate." If a word sounds like it would fit on a billboard, do not use it.

### What this calendar is trying to do

The 7 days are sequenced, not random. Each day has a job.

- **Days 1-3** establish the story and the credibility. No pitch, no link, no CTA. Just the human and the science.
- **Day 4** is the first post that talks about the build honestly. Honesty is the brand.
- **Day 5** is the value drop. A real cheat sheet people will screenshot and share. This is the post that gets the most saves and the most reach.
- **Day 6** explains the differentiator. "Adaptive audio" is a vague phrase. The AI Coach makes it concrete.
- **Day 7** is a week-one retro. The numbers are real (or close to it). The lessons are honest. This is where the audience learns you are a person, not a campaign.

If a post feels off-tone, do not post it. Trust the voice. The voice is the only moat.

## Day 3 — LinkedIn long-form [L]

- **Platform:** LinkedIn
- **Format:** Long-form (about 470 words)
- **Time:** 08:00 Europe/Berlin

> I am going to tell you the math behind Rulio's pricing. It is the question I have been asked the most by the 6 people I have told about this product so far, and I have rewritten this answer 4 times.
>
> Rulio is €19 a month, or €180 a year. The "obvious" alternative, the one every growth person on the internet would tell me to use, is €9 a month. A nice round number. Easier to say yes to. Half the price.
>
> Here is why I did not pick €9.
>
> The first reason is the user. I am building Rulio for people who have tried everything else. People who have already bought the €9 apps, the €4.99 apps, the "free with ads" apps, the Headspace trial, the Calm trial. They churned out of those products. They left because the product was not built for them. The €9 price point is the price point of a product they have already quit. Charging €9 tells them Rulio is the same shape as the thing they left.
>
> The second reason is the company. I am one person. I have to pay for the audio engine, the database, the email service, the bandwidth, the legal bits, and the small amount of money I pay myself. At €9 a month, after payment processing, I make about €8.40 per user per month. To pay myself a Belgian junior salary, I need around 1,200 active users. At €19 a month, after processing, I make about €17.80 per user. I need around 570. The second number is reachable. The first is not. The first number would mean I quit and go back to a job.
>
> The third reason is the year. The annual plan is €180. That is €15 a month if you do the math. So if you want the €9 feeling, it is there, just on a 12-month commitment. I would rather you pay €180 once than €9 twelve times and feel nothing.
>
> The fourth reason is the trial. Rulio is free for 7 days with no card. I will never ask for a card on the free tier. If after 7 days you do not feel something, you leave with nothing lost. The trial is the answer to "but I am not sure it is worth €19." Try it. If it is not worth it, you have not paid.
>
> I am aware that some of you reading this are founders, and you are going to tell me that I should A/B test this. You are right. I will. I just do not think the test will move the number, because the number is not the product. The product is the practice. The number is the price of sticking with the practice for 30 days.
>
> If you want to try the 7-day free trial, no card: rulio.app/pro

- **Platform:** LinkedIn
- **Format:** Long-form (about 460 words)
- **Time:** 08:00 Europe/Berlin

> One week ago I launched Rulio. Here are the numbers and the three lessons I learned.
>
> The numbers, in the spirit of not rounding up:
>
> - [X] people started the free tier
> - [X] made it past day 3 of the practice
> - [X] converted to a paid plan
> - [X] wrote me a real email (not a spam report, an actual human message)
> - 1 person in Berlin wrote me a 4-paragraph email about how the 285 Hz session helped their post-marathon recovery. That email is the reason I am doing this.
>
> I will not pretend these are hockey-stick numbers. They are small. They are the small numbers of a product launched by one person in Brussels with no ad budget and a small but loyal group of friends who reposted things in the first 24 hours.
>
> Three lessons, all of which I wish I had known on Day 1.
>
> Lesson 1: The retention cliff is on day 2. The people who convert are the people who listen to a session on day 1, and then listen to another one on the same day. The people who "plan to use it tomorrow" almost never come back. This is the same lesson every consumer app has learned, and I am embarrassed to say I had to learn it again from scratch.
>
> Lesson 2: The 528 Hz post is the only one that broke out of my own network. It got 3x the impressions of every other post. People search for "528 Hz" the way they used to search for "binaural beats." I am going to write a proper, evidence-led deep-dive on it next week, because the demand is real even if the science is soft.
>
> Lesson 3: The no-card trial converts at a lower rate than a card-required trial, but the no-card trial users stay on longer. I had considered switching to a card-required trial in week 1 because the conversion number was lower than I hoped. I did not. I am glad I did not. The people who come in through a no-card trial are the people who tell their friends. The people who come in through a card-required trial are the people who forget to cancel.
>
> What changes in week 2:
>
> - A proper 528 Hz deep-dive post
> - An email to the people who downloaded the free tier but did not convert, asking what stopped them
