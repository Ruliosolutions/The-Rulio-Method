No. 02 — The three apps, honestly

What they do well. What they don't.

Both Calm and Headspace are legitimate products with millions of users. These sections name what each is genuinely good at — and where they don't fit someone who needs something different.

C
Calm
Sleep stories, broad lifestyle library, long sessions.

Calm is the largest consumer meditation app by revenue, with a content catalogue that goes well beyond meditation: narrated sleep stories (Idris Elba, Harry Styles, Laura Dern), music, masterclasses with therapists, and a daily "Calm" video. It's a great general-purpose wellness product, and the sleep stories are genuinely good.

What it does well

  • Sleep stories — the deepest library on the market, with celebrity narrators.
  • Wide content variety: meditations, music, soundscapes, masterclasses.
  • Polished UI; it's the most "consumer" of the three apps.
  • Apple TV + Apple Watch integration for sleep tracking.
  • Kids section with full children content.
  • Daily Calm — a fresh ~10-minute guided session every day.

What it doesn't do

  • No frequency-based audio. It's all narration and music.
  • Sessions skew long (10–45 min), hard to fit between meetings.
  • Free tier is restrictive — full library requires paid plan.
  • No custom sessions or build-your-own protocols.
  • No AI coach (text-based interaction).
  • US-based; data residency is US by default.

Who it's for

  • Light sleepers who enjoy the sleep-story format.
  • People who want one app for sleep + meditation + lifestyle.
  • Families (the kids library is excellent).
  • Users who prefer polished narrated content over silence.
  • Anyone new to meditation who benefits from clear verbal guidance.
H
Headspace
Themed courses, habit formation, the most structured of the three.

Headspace started as a meditation app built by a former Buddhist monk (Andy Puddicombe). It's the most pedagogical of the three apps — think courses, themed weeks, and habit-formation streaks. In 2024 it added "Ebb," a CBT-style AI text companion. It's a good product if you want to learn meditation as a discipline over weeks.

What it does well

  • Best "Basics" course in the category — 10 free sessions that actually teach meditation.
  • Structured courses: "The Mindful Eating Course," "Anxiety" course, etc.
  • Family plan (up to 6) at a competitive price.
  • Strong habit-formation UX (streaks, daily reminders).
  • Best-in-class for workplace / B2B partnerships.
  • Sleepcasts are solid, though shorter than Calm's stories.

What it doesn't do

  • No frequency-based audio or solfeggio framework.
  • Shortest sessions are still ~10 minutes; no under-5 option.
  • Content library is more curated than Calm; less "explore the catalogue" feel.
  • Ebb (the AI coach) is text-based, not aware of audio sessions.
  • No custom protocols or build-your-own frequency sessions.
  • US-based; limited EU data residency options.

Who it's for

  • Beginners who want a structured 30-day curriculum.
  • People who respond well to gentle British-accent voiceover.
  • Workplace mental-health buyers (strong B2B story).
  • Parents who want the family plan.
  • Users building a daily streak they care about.
R
Rulio
Solfeggio frequencies. 5-minute protocol. AI coach. EU-hosted.

Rulio was built for a specific gap: most audio apps assume you have 20–45 minutes and want a voice in your ear. Rulio assumes the opposite — that you have 5 minutes, no patience for narration, and want the frequency itself to do the work. All nine solfeggio frequencies, layered with binaural beats and a frequency-aware AI coach, in sessions you can run between two meetings.

What it does well

  • All 9 solfeggio frequencies (174 – 963 Hz) as a core feature, not a gimmick.
  • 5-minute minimum session — the shortest in the comparison.
  • Build-your-own protocols: pick frequency, binaural offset, duration.
  • AI coach that's frequency-aware — it knows what's playing and prompts based on context.
  • EU-hosted (Frankfurt), Brussels-built, founder-led.
  • Flat €19/mo — no surprise upsells, no family tier gatekeeping.

What it doesn't do (yet)

  • No narrated guided meditations. Rulio is audio-only.
  • No celebrity sleep stories. The "sleep drift" preset is 60 min of layered frequencies.
  • No kids content, no family plan (yet).
  • No native iOS / Android app yet (web only; iOS Q4 2026).
  • No Apple TV, no Apple Watch app.
  • No B2B / workplace plan.

Who it's for

  • Founders, operators, engineers — anyone who needs focus between meetings.
  • People who find guided narration distracting rather than helpful.
  • Users curious about solfeggio frequencies as a structured practice.
  • EU-based people who care about data residency.
  • Anyone who wants a founder they can email (Roel reads every message).
  • Solo builders who'd rather pay €19 than commit to a 12-month family plan.
No. 03 — Founder's note

Why Rulio exists.

Three paragraphs. No pitch.

I built Rulio because I couldn't stick with Calm or Headspace. Not because they're bad — they're not. Because they assumed I had 30 minutes and wanted someone narrating at me. I had neither. What I had was a 14-hour day, three meetings between noon and three, and the need to be sharp for each one. The apps kept trying to give me the kind of wellness content that assumes your evening is free.

The pattern that actually worked, when I tried it, was five minutes of a single solfeggio frequency at a specific binaural-beat offset — nothing else. No voice, no music, no story. Sit down, headphones on, breathe once, five minutes, stand up. The difference between that and a 30-minute guided session isn't smaller. It fits the day I actually have, not the day I wish I had. Five minutes is the only unit of time a founder has between commitments.

Rulio is the product I'd have wanted two years ago: the 9 solfeggio frequencies, layered properly, with an AI coach that knows what's playing and can prompt based on what frequency you chose. No library of 800 sleep stories. No "start your 30-day streak" nags. Just a well-built tool for the gap between meetings, run by one person in Brussels who reads the support inbox. If you're not the target user, Calm and Headspace are honestly good — use them. Rulio exists for the people they're not built for.

No. 05 — FAQ

Questions people actually search.

Drawn from real search queries. Direct answers, no marketing.

Is Rulio a Calm alternative?
Yes — if you're looking for frequency-based audio instead of guided meditation. Rulio is built around the 9 solfeggio frequencies with 5-minute sessions between meetings; Calm is built around 10–30 minute sleep stories and a wide content library. If you want short, structured audio without narration, Rulio is the same category. If you want long-form sleep stories narrated by celebrities, Calm is the better fit.

Both have free tiers. Calm paid starts at €14.99/mo; Rulio is €19/mo. The price difference reflects what Rulio is doing with frequencies and the AI coach, not feature bloat.
Is Rulio better than Headspace for anxiety?
Different tool, different mechanism. Headspace uses guided CBT-style meditation and short talks. Rulio uses the 9 solfeggio frequencies (174 Hz – 963 Hz) with binaural-beat offsets and an optional AI coach. Neither app is a medical device, and neither is a clinical treatment for anxiety. If you respond to audio-only sessions under 10 minutes, Rulio's 5-minute protocol is the more useful tool. If you prefer long, narrated, teacher-led sessions, Headspace is the better fit.

For clinical anxiety we recommend a licensed clinician. Both apps are wellness tools.
What is the cheapest meditation app among the three?
All three have free tiers. Among paid plans as of September 2026: Headspace starts at €12.99/mo, Calm at €14.99/mo, Rulio at €19/mo. Rulio is the most expensive, but it's the only one built specifically around solfeggio frequencies and 5-minute structured sessions. Annual plans reduce the per-month cost on all three.
Does Calm or Headspace use solfeggio frequencies?
No. As of 2026, neither Calm nor Headspace features the solfeggio frequency scale (174, 285, 396, 417, 528, 639, 741, 852, 963 Hz) as a core layer. Rulio is built around these nine frequencies and lets you combine them with binaural-beat offsets and an AI coach for targeted protocols. If "solfeggio frequency app" was your search query, this is the comparison page you were looking for.
Can I cancel Rulio anytime?
Yes. Rulio Engine Pro is a monthly subscription with a 7-day free trial that does not require a credit card. You can cancel from your account settings at any time. There is no annual lock-in, no cancellation fee, no phone tree.
Where is Rulio hosted?
Rulio runs on Supabase, hosted in the Frankfurt (EU-Central) region. Audio files, account data, and payment metadata are stored within the EU under GDPR. Rulio is built by one founder in Brussels. EU residency matters if you'd rather your audio and account data not transit the US by default.
Is Rulio a medical device?
No. Rulio is not a medical device, is not intended to diagnose, treat, cure, or prevent any disease or condition, and is not a substitute for medical advice. It is a wellness and focus audio tool. If you are experiencing acute anxiety, depression, or other mental health concerns, please consult a licensed clinician. Calm and Headspace carry equivalent disclaimers.
How long is a Rulio session?
Rulio sessions start at 3 minutes. The most common templates are 5–12 minutes, with the shortest focused on a single frequency. The longest preset template is the 60-minute sleep drift (174 Hz + 528 Hz at low binaural offset). You can build your own protocol in Engine Pro and save it as a preset.
